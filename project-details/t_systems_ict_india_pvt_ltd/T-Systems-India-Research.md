## 1. Executive Summary

T-Systems ICT India is the **Indian delivery and capability arm of Deutsche Telekom's IT services subsidiary**. It is simultaneously two things that are usually separate:

1. **A captive Global Capability Centre (GCC)** delivering for its German parent and, increasingly, for other Deutsche Telekom group entities.
2. **A third-party IT and cloud services provider** serving 150+ external global customers across automotive, manufacturing, public sector, healthcare, financial services and retail.

FY2025 results make it one of the more profitable pieces of the T-Systems group:

| Metric | FY2025 |
|---|---|
| Revenue | ₹1,413.1 Cr (~€150M / ~$170M) |
| Net profit (PAT) | ₹164.3 Cr |
| Net margin | **11.6%** |
| EBITDA | ₹302.8 Cr |
| EBITDA margin | **21.4%** |
| Revenue growth | 8.2% YoY |
| 3-year revenue CAGR | 26% |
| 5-year revenue CAGR | 28% |
| Employees (EPFO) | 4,835 |
| Revenue per employee | ~₹2.92M (~$33k) |

The decisive analytical point: **India runs at roughly twice the group's margin.** T-Systems group reports ~€4.1B revenue and ~€0.4B adjusted EBITDA AL (~9–10% margin). India's entity posts a 21.4% EBITDA margin. That is why the group strategy explicitly targets **80% delivery offshoring by 2027e** and India is the single largest near-/offshore block in the group (3,908–4,835 people depending on definition).

The company is now ten years old (Pune PoP opened September 2016 with 100 people; September 2026 marked 5,000+ people across Pune, Bengaluru and Hyderabad).

## 3. What T-Systems India Actually Does

### 3.1 The dual mandate
This is the single most important structural fact about the entity.

**As a captive GCC:** India is the group's primary offshore delivery engine. It runs application management, cloud operations, SAP, infrastructure, cybersecurity and AI/GenAI engineering for T-Systems' own customers, largely German and European enterprises. The group's stated ambition is **80% delivery offshoring by 2027e** — India is the vehicle.

**As a third-party provider:** India also sells directly to 150+ global customers, competes in Indian and international vendor evaluations, and carries its own P&L with a board and MD. This makes it a hybrid that most GCCs are not: it has external revenue, external competitive pressure, and external partner relationships.

### 3.2 Service portfolio (India site)
- **Application Services** — SAP Solutions (S/4HANA), ServiceNow, Enterprise Application Services, Landscape Modernization
- **Application Modernization** — consulting, migration, refactor/containerize, managed services
- **Cloud Services** — cloud consulting & transformation, migration, infrastructure, managed services (T Cloud, AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, VMware)
- **Artificial Intelligence** — AI Solution Factory, AI & Data Analytics, AI Foundation Services, Industrial AI Cloud, AI on Google Cloud, UiPath AI-as-a-Service
- **Security** — cyber defence, cloud security, data/application security, GRC, IAM, industrial/IoT security, network & infrastructure security, AI & emerging-tech security
- **Data Intelligence** — Data Intelligence Hub, big data analytics, data spaces, modern backup, data mesh, machine data analytics, Digital Product Passport
- **Intelligent Automation** — hyperautomation
- **IoT & Edge** — IoT solutions, remote machine monitoring, EdgAir
- **Quantum Computing** — Quantum as a Service
- **Strategy & Consulting** — operations & performance, technology, transformation & innovation, industries consulting
- **Content & Collaboration** — enterprise content management

### 3.3 Industry verticals
Automotive (connected mobility, in-car software, OTA, SDV, Catena-X, NVIDIA Omniverse, battery production), Manufacturing (PLM, SAP S/4HANA, AI-based production optimisation), Public Sector & Public Transport (digital administration, rail, smart stations, MaaS, airport management), Healthcare (clinical applications, health data management, telematics infrastructure), Financial Services (sovereign cloud, core modernisation, DORA-aligned resilience, quantum readiness), Retail/Logistics/Utilities, Chemicals & Pharmaceuticals.

## 7. Strategy: "One T", AI@T, and the Offshore Thesis

### 7.1 Group-level strategy that drives India
Deutsche Telekom's "Leading Digital Telco" strategy has six pillars, two of which directly determine India's mandate:
- **#3 Increase productivity and cost efficiency end-to-end** through continuous automation, simplification and modernisation.
- **#5 Become a data-informed, AI-enabled, digital-first company — AI@T.**

T-Systems' own stated levers:
- **80% delivery offshoring by 2027e** (up from current levels)
- **10,000 people reskilled in AI** by 2027
- **20% efficiency gain through automation**, especially AI
- **10,000 near-/offshore** out of ~25,000 total
- **1,500+ AI experts** group-wide

### 7.2 T-Systems India's role in that strategy
India is where three of the five levers physically happen:
1. **Offshoring** — Pune, Bengaluru and Hyderabad absorb the European delivery base.
2. **Reskilling** — the 10,000-AI-reskilling target is executed substantially in India.
3. **Automation** — the 20% efficiency target is delivered by India-based engineering.
4. **AI productisation** — India builds agentic AI, data and cloud engineering for group customers.
5. **Cost** — India's 21.4% EBITDA margin funds group investment.

### 7.3 The "One T" argument
T-Systems positions "One T" as its competitive advantage: a single global B2B setup (~€15B B2B FTE, 40k+ people, 5M+ customer accounts, 5% service revenue growth) that combines German sovereignty and compliance credibility with Indian delivery economics. **India is the cost and scale half of that argument.** Without India, "One T" is a European services company with German cost structures.

### 7.4 Sovereign AI as the strategic wedge
Q1 2026: Europe's first **Industrial AI Cloud** with 10,000 NVIDIA Blackwell DGX B200 GPUs, built in six months, positioned as sovereign European AI compute. November 2026: Deutsche Telekom expanding the Munich AI factory to 20 megawatts.

India's role here is not infrastructure (that stays in Germany) but **engineering, integration, model operations and application delivery**. This is the highest-value work the group has, and India is positioned to take a meaningful share.

### 7.5 India-as-GCC advisory
T-Systems India has been building a **secondary business advising German Mittelstand on setting up their own India GCCs** — through Detecon, IGCC workshops and NASSCOM channels. This is strategically clever: it monetises T-Systems' 10 years of India operating experience as a consulting product, and it creates relationships with German companies that may later become T-Systems delivery clients.

### 8.2 The critical market signal for T-Systems India
**GCC hiring volumes fell 28% between H1 and H2 FY2026.** Revenue CAGR (~9.9%) is outrunning talent CAGR (~6.2%). The market is decoupling output from headcount.

This cuts both ways for T-Systems India:
- **Threat:** If Indian IT services demand softens and GCCs hire less, captive delivery centres face volume pressure and rate compression.
- **Opportunity:** A 28% hiring drop means **talent is easier to hire and cheaper to retain**. T-Systems India, at 10% attrition and restarting its fresher program, is well positioned to absorb available supply while competitors retrench.

### 8.3 German MNC context
- Indian labour costs are 50–70% lower than Germany; a 500-person German GCC can save up to €275M over five years (NASSCOM/Inductus).
- India produces ~2.5M STEM graduates annually.
- EMEA-origin GCCs skew toward business process management (~31%), unlike Americas-origin GCCs which skew product/platform.
- T-Systems India is unusual among EMEA GCCs because it also has **external third-party revenue** — it is a GCC and a vendor at once.

## 10. Risk Assessment

### 10.1 Structural / parent-level
1. **Parent decline.** T-Systems headcount fell from ~46,000 (2023) to ~25,000–26,000 (2025). Group revenue is flat to declining (Q2 2026 revenue -4.3%, EBITDA AL -9.3% YoY). India's growth is partly an offset to a shrinking parent, which is not a durable formula.
2. **Customer concentration.** As a captive, a large share of India's revenue derives from Deutsche Telekom group entities. This is stable but not diversified, and it limits the entity's standalone valuation.
3. **Transfer-pricing exposure.** The 21.4% EBITDA margin versus the group's ~10% invites scrutiny from Indian tax authorities on arm's-length pricing of intra-group services.

### 10.2 Market / competitive
4. **Indian IT services softening.** Discretionary spending is constrained; Q1 FY2027 saw TCS, Infosys and HCLTech all miss revenue estimates on weak BFSI and retail budgets. T-Systems India's 8.2% FY2025 growth already reflects deceleration from 26% CAGR.
5. **GCC hiring contraction (-28% H2 FY26).** If captive demand softens, T-Systems India's external third-party pipeline must carry more weight — a business it has not fully built.
6. **Rate compression.** Indian IT services pricing power is eroding; AI is being used by clients as a lever to demand lower rates for the same scope.

### 10.3 Talent / organisational
7. **Career-progression sentiment (3.3/5).** The single biggest driver of senior attrition. At 10% overall attrition this is tolerable; at 15% it becomes a delivery risk.
8. **Pune concentration.** 57 of 72 open roles are in Pune. Single-city concentration creates real-estate, wage-inflation and attrition-correlation risk.
9. **Recruitment practice risk.** A September 2026 public complaint alleges a rescinded offer after verbal confirmation and three technical rounds, citing "disorganized headcount planning." Reputational damage in a talent market where employer brand is a stated priority.
10. **AI displacing entry level.** The company restarted a 12-month fresher program in 2025 — the right move — but the industry-wide pattern is that AI is removing exactly the junior roles that program feeds.

### 10.4 Compliance / regulatory
11. **EU regulatory cascade.** DORA (operational resilience for financial entities), NIS2, the EU AI Act, and the German Supply Chain Act (LkSG) all increase the compliance burden on delivery. T-Systems has a dedicated DORA page, so it is prepared — but this is a rising cost line.
12. **India DPDP Act 2023.** Rules notified in late 2025 impose cross-border transfer restrictions and consent obligations. As a processor of European client data delivered from India, this requires contractual and architectural work.
13. **Sovereignty tension.** European clients want European data residency; Indian delivery requires data access. The "sovereign cloud + Indian engineering" model works, but the data-flow design is the hard part.

- **Decade of India operating experience** — 100 → 5,000+ people, three cities, 150+ customers, 85% agile delivery.

- **Parent in decline** and India-dependent for margin offset.
- **Single-client concentration** in group revenue.
- **Career progression (3.3/5) and senior management (3.0/5)** sentiment.
- **No India brand presence** among Indian enterprise buyers.
- **No India-specific newsroom output** — marketing is under-leveraged.
- **Narrow portfolio** relative to Indian majors.
- **Pune-heavy footprint** and modest Hyderabad scale.

- **Sovereign AI** — Industrial AI Cloud and the Munich AI factory create high-value engineering demand that India can capture.
- **SAP S/4HANA wave through 2027** — 8 open SAP roles today, a multi-year runway.

### Threats
- **Indian IT services demand softening** and rate compression.
- **AI-driven scope reduction** in application maintenance and testing — the core captive workload.

- **Geopolitical / regulatory** — EU AI Act, DORA, DPDP compliance cost; India-EU data flow politics.
- **Parent strategy risk** — if T-Systems pivots further toward sovereign cloud and away from managed services, India's application workload shrinks.

## 12. Strategic Outlook

### Near term (2026–2027)
- Continue absorbing European delivery volume as the 80% offshoring target is executed.
- Scale SAP S/4HANA and cloud migration delivery out of Pune and Bengaluru.
- Grow agentic AI engineering — the live job feed shows 4 AI roles including "Agentic AI Developer," which is the tell.
- Maintain ~10% attrition and keep the fresher pipeline running.
- Convert India GCC advisory relationships (Detecon, IGCC) into delivery mandates.

### Medium term (2027–2029)
- **India becomes the group's default delivery model**, not a cost-arbitrage option. The strategic narrative shifts from "send work to India" to "build in India."
- **AI engineering displaces application maintenance** as the dominant India workload. This is the make-or-break transition: if India only does migration and maintenance while AI engineering stays in Germany, the offshoring thesis stalls.
- **Hyderabad scales** to reduce Pune concentration.
- **External third-party revenue grows** as a share of India's P&L, reducing captive dependence.

### The decisive question
T-Systems India's economics are excellent and its group mandate is secure. The risk is not competitive — it is **strategic obsolescence**. The work that justifies 80% offshoring is precisely the work AI is automating: application maintenance, testing, infrastructure operations, service desk.

If T-Systems India successfully repositioned from "cost arbitrage delivery" to "AI engineering and sovereign AI operations," it becomes the most valuable asset in the T-Systems group. If it does not, it becomes a well-run, profitable, slowly shrinking captive — the fate that has befallen much of the traditional Indian IT services base.

The 2026 evidence is mixed but leans positive: 16 architect roles, 4 AI roles, a German-language niche, a sovereign AI platform to build on, and a restarted graduate pipeline. The counter-evidence is an 8.2% growth rate after a 28% CAGR, a parent in structural decline, and career-progression sentiment at 3.3/5.

- EBITDA and margin comparisons between the India entity and the group are affected by intra-group transfer pricing and should be read directionally, not as an audited like-for-like.

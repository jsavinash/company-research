## 1. Executive Summary

Nestlé USA is the US arm of the world's largest food and beverage company, and the United States is Nestlé's single largest market — roughly **32% of group sales** and the anchor of Zone Americas.

The US business is organised as three distinct businesses under a common holding company:

| Business | What it is | Approx. role |
|---|---|---|
| **Nestlé USA** | Grocery, coffee, creamers, baking, frozen and chilled | The heritage CPG core (31 brands) |
| **Nespresso USA** | Portioned coffee, machines, boutiques, e-commerce | Globally managed premium coffee |
| **Nestlé Purina PetCare** | Dog/cat food, litter, treats | Largest US pet food franchise |

**Financial scale (Nestlé Holdings, Inc. — the US holding company):**
- 2025 sales: **US$30,863M** (2024: US$31,171M)
- H1 2026 sales: **US$16,862M** (H1 2025: US$14,818M), +13.8%
- US market sales at group level (FY2025): **CHF 28,605M ≈ US$32.5B**, 32.0% of group

**The strategic situation:** Nestlé USA is mid-turnaround. A new US CEO (Marty Thompson, since 1 Jan 2025) is conducting an "extensive review" of a 31-brand portfolio, running blind taste tests across three-quarters of the portfolio, restructuring divisions, and preparing for further divestitures. The parent is simultaneously executing a group-wide restructuring (up to 16,000 roles, 2026–2027) and a cost programme ("Fuel for Growth") targeted at a CHF 2.0B annual savings run-rate.

The market backdrop is unusually hostile for packaged food: flat-to-negative volumes, record private-label share, cocoa and coffee cost shocks, tariff volatility, and the first measurable GLP-1-driven shifts in grocery baskets. Nestlé's Q3 2026 shows genuine progress — US organic growth accelerated, frozen brands gained share, coffee recovered after price increases — but Gerber and Nutrition remain weak.

## 5. Strategy and Turnaround

### 5.1 Nestlé USA: Thompson's playbook
Marty Thompson took over on 1 January 2025 with an explicit mandate to accelerate US growth. His stated approach (Dec 2025 interview):

1. **Portfolio review** — an "extensive review" of the US portfolio, with "a sense of urgency."
2. **Blind taste tests** — Nestlé began testing its products against competitors (e.g., DiGiorno vs Red Baron and Domino's) to decide on reformulation. Target: three-quarters of the portfolio tested by end-March 2026.
3. **Fewer, bigger innovation bets** — concentrate resources on the core rather than spreading thin.
4. **Packaging re-architecture** — rethink pack sizes; e.g., a single-count Hot Pockets pack to attract new buyers.
5. **Price-value rebalancing** — explicitly "rebalanced" pizza pricing to regain share.
6. **Divisional restructuring** — merged pizza, snacking and meals into one division; merged coffee and creamers.
7. **More divestitures** — underperforming brands on the block.

**Early results (as of Q3 2026):**
- Frozen brands (Stouffer's, DiGiorno, Hot Pockets) delivering **positive market share** despite a challenging category.
- Creamers business in "strong turnaround" after the coffee/creamer merger.
- Coffee RIG recovering progressively after the initial impact of earlier price increases.
- Confectionery in Zone Americas growing with share gains.
- Gerber and Nutrition still "underperforming our potential."

### 5.2 Nestlé S.A.: Navratil's restructuring
Philipp Navratil became group CEO in September 2025, succeeding Laurent Freixe (dismissed over an undisclosed relationship with a subordinate). His agenda:

- **October 2025:** announced up to **16,000 job reductions over 2026–2027** (~6% of 277,000 employees), comprising ~12,000 office and ~4,000 factory roles. Reported as part of an effort to drive ~US$3.8B (≈CHF 3.5B) in savings by end-2027.
- **"Fuel for Growth"** — the cost programme: CHF 1.1B savings in 2025, +CHF 0.9B incremental in 2026, reaching a **CHF 2.0B annual run-rate**.
- **Portfolio sharpening** — divest the mainstream VMS business (US$1.0B), sharpen around categories with the strongest competitive advantage.
- **Growth investment** — increased advertising and marketing to 8.6% of sales in 2025.
- **Russia** — the Russian business was placed under temporary external administration and is **no longer consolidated** from end-September 2026.

Thompson has confirmed the cuts "will affect every market and every function in some way" in the US, and that Nestlé USA is "on the front end of that."

### 5.3 The strategic logic
The through-line is simple: Nestlé USA has too many small brands and not enough investment in its big ones. The turnaround is a classic CPG portfolio-triage:
- Concentrate investment on the 4–5 US$1B+ brands.
- Fix the price-value equation where share has been lost.
- Rebuild organisational alignment with how consumers shop (one frozen division, merged coffee/creamers).
- Fund the reinvestment from overhead and tail-brand divestitures.

### 5.4 The strategic risk
The risk is that this is a **price and mix** strategy in a **volume** market. US food volumes per capita fell 0.6% in H1 2026. If Nestlé USA wins share in frozen and coffee while the category shrinks, it improves relative position without restoring absolute growth. That is still valuable — it is what public markets reward in a flat category — but it caps the ceiling.

## 8. Market Context: The US Food and Beverage Environment (2026)

This is the most important section for understanding Nestlé USA's results.

### 8.1 Volumes are flat to negative
- US food and non-alcoholic beverage **volume per person fell 0.6%** in H1 2026 (BEA).
- Circana's revised 2026 outlook: **price/mix +2% to +4%, volume flat or slightly negative.**
- USD retail food and drink sales rose 2.2% in H1 2026 — entirely price and mix.

**Implication:** Nestlé USA cannot grow on category expansion. Growth must come from share gain, price/mix, or new usage occasions.

### 8.2 Private label at record share
- US store brands reached a record **23.8% of units** in the six months to June 2026 (Circana).
- Private label crossed **US$330B** in annual sales, ~24% of value share.
- Store brand units grew 0.2% while national brands fell 0.5%.
- 65% of consumer products executives expect private-label competition to intensify further over the next three years.

**Implication:** This is the central structural threat to every Nestlé USA brand. Coffee mate, Nesquik, Toll House and DiGiorno all face private-label competition at meaningfully lower prices with converging quality.

### 8.3 GLP-1 drugs — real but smaller than forecast
The data now allows a sober assessment:
- US adults using GLP-1 drugs: **11% (Gallup, 2026)** to **18% (FTI Consulting, spring 2026)** — up from ~3–4% in 2024.
- Cornell/Numerator study (150,000 households): grocery spending falls **5.3%** within six months of starting; higher-income households −8%; savoury snacks −10.1%; fast food −8.0%.
- The effect **eases after six months**, and about a third of users stop taking the drugs, after which spending reverts.
- **Frozen foods show the largest CPG category impact: a 3-point drop in dollar spend over year one of active use** (FTI).
- National grocery bill impact: roughly **0.5%–1%**, not the 5–10% some forecasts projected.
- EY-Parthenon: up to **US$12B in snack sales lost over the next decade**.

**Implication for Nestlé USA:** Frozen is the most exposed category — DiGiorno, Stouffer's, Hot Pockets and Lean Cuisine all sit in it. This is a direct, measurable headwind, and it is the category where Nestlé USA is investing most.

### 8.4 Commodity shocks: cocoa, coffee, tariffs
- **Cocoa**: peaked near **US$12,000/tonne** (Apr/Dec 2024); collapsed to ~**US$3,500/tonne** by Feb 2026. Nestlé and peers are hedged, so the benefit lags 12–18 months.
- **Coffee**: extreme volatility, now stabilising at elevated levels.
- **Tariffs**: US tariff policy caused "trade policy turmoil" through 2025; rollbacks on coffee, cocoa and other imported ingredients in late 2025 provided relief; some tariffs remain very high (e.g., 107% on Italian products cited).
- **USDA**: all-food prices forecast to rise **3.0% in 2026**; grocery prices +2.5%.
- PPI for all foods stood **34% above** its February 2020 level as of September 2026.

**Implication:** Nestlé USA's 2025 pricing (+2.8% group) was driven by coffee and cocoa inflation. As cocoa deflates, the pricing lever weakens and the volume lever becomes the only path — which is exactly what the frozen and coffee recovery attempts.

### 8.5 The consumer
- US consumer confidence remains weak; low- and middle-income households are "increasingly challenged."
- Shoppers are trading down to private label in staples while selectively trading up in premium categories ("selective trade-up").
- Aldi plans 180+ new US stores in 2026, reaching ~2,800 by year-end and 3,200 by 2028, with >90% own-brand products.

### 9.2 Competitive position
- **Purina** is the strongest business: #1 or #2 in US pet food, a 30% share of Zone Americas sales, and the only Nestlé USA category consistently gaining share. Pet care remains the anchor.
- **Coffee** is a genuine global leader (Nescafé, Nespresso, Starbucks CPG) but is exposed to JDE Peet's and Keurig Dr Pepper in the US.
- **Frozen** is where Nestlé is #1 in pizza (DiGiorno) but faces Schwan's, Conagra and aggressive private label, plus GLP-1 volume pressure.
- **Nutrition (Gerber)** is the weak spot: "underperformed our potential" and lost share in Q3 2026.

### 9.3 Competitive advantage
Nestlé's durable advantages in the US:
1. **Distribution breadth** — 98% household penetration; presence in grocery, mass, club, dollar, convenience, e-commerce and foodservice.
2. **Scale in frozen and coffee** — enough volume to fund innovation and shelf presence.
3. **Pet care leadership** — Purina's vet-channel and brand portfolio is hard to replicate.
4. **R&D and culinary capability** — the NC Innovation & Technology Center and a large chef/culinary team.
5. **Balance sheet** — the parent can fund a US turnaround through a cyclical downturn.

Nestlé's disadvantages:
1. **No unique product truth** in most categories — frozen pizza and creamers are not defensible on technology.
2. **Slower NPD cycles** (18–36 months) than digitally native challengers.
3. **Price premium eroding** against converging private label.
4. **Brand proliferation** — 31 brands is a lot to support with a finite marketing budget.

## 11. Risk Assessment

### 11.1 Market and demand
1. **Volume decline.** US food volumes per capita are falling (0.6% H1 2026). Nestlé USA's growth is share- and price-led, not category-led. If private label keeps taking units, the ceiling is structural.
2. **Private label at record share.** 23.8% of units and rising; quality convergence means Nestlé's price premium is harder to defend every year.
3. **GLP-1 volume pressure in frozen.** Frozen shows the largest CPG impact (3-point dollar-spend drop in year one of use). Nestlé's single largest US investment category is the most exposed.
4. **Consumer weakness.** Low- and middle-income households are trading down; Aldi and discounters are expanding aggressively.
5. **Commodity whiplash.** Cocoa has collapsed from ~$12,000/t to ~$3,500/t; coffee remains elevated and volatile. Hedging means Nestlé benefits late and suffers early.

### 11.2 Company-specific
6. **Portfolio overhang.** 31 brands is too many for the investment available. The review could result in more divestitures, but each divestiture removes scale and complicates the "one Nestlé" story.
7. **Gerber and Nutrition.** "Underperformed our potential" — a persistent drag with share loss. Gerber is 100+ years old and facing demographic and trust headwinds.
8. **Execution risk in the turnaround.** The turnaround depends on fewer, bigger innovation bets and taste-test-driven reformulation. If those bets miss, the company has both lost share and reduced its pipeline.
9. **Restructuring distraction.** The US business is being asked to deliver growth while absorbing group-level job cuts and portfolio changes. Thompson himself called the US "on the front end" of the reductions.
10. **Parent-level capital allocation.** Nestlé's net debt is CHF 51.4B and gearing 155%. The group has finite capacity to fund US growth; a downturn could force prioritisation elsewhere.

### 11.3 Regulatory and reputational
11. **Tariffs.** US tariff policy remains volatile. Nestlé's own reporting cites tariff impacts on working capital and costs; rollbacks have helped but are reversible.
12. **Food regulation.** EU AI Act, EUDR (deforestation regulation), front-of-pack labelling, sodium and sugar reduction targets, and US FDA labelling rules all raise reformulation cost.
13. **Infant formula recall.** The 2025 infant formula recall cost CHF 75M in sales returns and CHF 110M in inventory write-offs, with a further ~20 bps impact on 2026 group organic growth. Trust damage in infant nutrition is slow to repair.
14. **Labor relations.** The group's 16,000-role reduction has drawn union criticism (IUF, European Works Council) over process and job quality, and questions about whether savings are reinvested.

## 12. Strengths / Weaknesses / Opportunities / Threats

### Strengths
- **Scale**: 32% of a CHF 89.5B group; US$30.9B NHI sales; 36,000 US employees; 98% household penetration.
- **Brand portfolio**: 4 US$1B+ brands and 5 US$500M+ brands, most #1 or #2 in their category.
- **Pet care**: Purina is a structural leader with pricing power and vet-channel credibility.
- **Frozen leadership**: DiGiorno is the #1 frozen pizza brand; the category has been a relative bright spot.
- **Supply chain investment**: US$25B over ten years; new automated DCs (Arvin, Glendale) and a new Purina plant.
- **Financial backing**: parent balance sheet can fund the turnaround through a weak category.
- **Employer brand**: Forbes Top US Employer 2026.

### Weaknesses
- **Over-diversified**: 31 brands, many sub-scale.
- **Volume exposure**: frozen and coffee are exposed to private label and GLP-1 shifts.
- **Weak Nutrition**: Gerber underperforming and losing share.
- **Slow innovation**: 18–36 month NPD cycles versus digitally native challengers.
- **Digital followership**: strong on Amazon, weak on first-party DTC.
- **Price premium eroding** against converging private label.

### Opportunities
- **Frozen innovation**: Wood Fired Style Crust, single-serve Hot Pockets, and price-value rebalancing are already regaining share.
- **Coffee and creamers integration**: cross-promotion between Coffee mate and Nescafé/Starbucks is showing results.
- **Purina premiumisation**: wet cat, Pro Plan and veterinary channels are growing mid-single-digit RIG.
- **Portion control / "snackification"**: GLP-1 users want smaller, more frequent, protein-forward formats — a natural fit for Hot Pockets and Lean Cuisine reformulation.
- **Premium water review**: Essentia and Sanpellegrino could be monetised at attractive valuations.
- **US data-centre and automation build-out**: Nestlé's own supply chain automation is a cost lever.
- **Emerging Hispanic market**: La Lechera, Media Crema, Abuelita, Carlos V and Nescafé address a fast-growing demographic.

### Threats
- **Private label at record share** and rising quality.
- **GLP-1 volume destruction in frozen and snacks.**
- **Cocoa and coffee volatility** (hedged, so lagging).
- **Tariff policy reversal risk.**
- **Aldi and discounter expansion.**
- **Challenger brands** in functional, protein-forward and better-for-you categories.
- **Restructuring execution and morale.**
- **Parent-level capital constraints** (CHF 51.4B net debt, 155% gearing).

## 13. Strategic Outlook

### Near term (2026–2027)
- Complete the US portfolio review; announce further divestitures (VMS closing H1 2027; Blue Bottle and premium water under review).
- Continue the frozen and coffee share-recovery trajectory; protect DiGiorno and Stouffer's.
- Absorb group restructuring impacts while delivering US organic growth above the US market.
- Deliver on the US$25B supply-chain commitment with further automation.
- Fix Gerber's trajectory — new kids hydration drink (Sept 2026) is the first visible move.
- Leverage the GLP-1 "portion control" trend with smaller formats and protein-forward innovation.

### Medium term (2028–2030)
- Nestlé USA emerges as a **narrower, higher-velocity portfolio**: coffee and creamers, frozen, baking, pet care.
- Group mid-term targets: 4%+ organic growth in normal markets, 17%+ UTOP margin, RIG 2%+.
- Continued premiumisation where it is real (coffee, pet) and value architecture where it is not (frozen, baking).
- Automation of the US supply chain to structurally lower cost per case.

### The decisive question
Nestlé USA is not a growth story in a growing market. It is a **share-defence and margin story in a flat market**.

The company's own numbers show it can do this: frozen share is improving, coffee RIG is recovering, Purina is gaining, and creamers have turned around. The risk is that these are **relative** wins in **absolute** decline — the categories themselves are shrinking, private label is taking units, and GLP-1 is structurally repricing frozen.

If Nestlé USA can convert a smaller portfolio into faster innovation and win the "portion control" occasion, it can hold its #1/#2 positions through the cycle. If it cannot, it becomes a well-managed cash generator funding growth elsewhere in the group — which, given the parent's debt load and the group's China and Europe challenges, is a real possibility.

The most telling metric to watch is not Nestlé USA's reported growth. It is **whether DiGiorno and Hot Pockets can grow units in a category where US frozen volume is structurally declining.** That single question determines whether the US turnaround is working or merely managing decline.

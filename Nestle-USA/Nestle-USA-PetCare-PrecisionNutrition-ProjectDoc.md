## 1. Summary

This document specifies a **precision pet nutrition and retention capability** for Purina: a system that builds a living profile for each individual pet (species, breed, age, weight, neuter status, activity, health conditions, allergies, current diet), recommends the right formula and daily portion, adapts as the pet ages or its health changes, and uses pet's uniquely fast repurchase cycle to run a retention and premiumization engine — with a veterinary-channel decision aid for prescription diets.

**Thesis:** Nestlé's durable advantage in pet is not product truth — Mars Petcare, General Mills (Blue Buffalo) and fresh-DTC challengers can match any kibble formula. It is **brand trust + the veterinary channel + Pro Plan's existing life-stage/condition segmentation + scale**, combined with pet's structural gift: **high-frequency repurchase and an emotionally invested owner**. A precision-nutrition engine that turns that into a continuously improving, vet-endorsed subscription relationship converts a transactional bag of food into recurring, premiumizing revenue — which is precisely the **real internal growth (RIG)** the group's mid-term target (sustainable 2%+ RIG) depends on. Pet is the one Nestlé USA unit structurally gaining share (§9.2); this project defends and monetises that lead rather than merely reporting it.

**Why this, why now:** Pet is 30% of Zone Americas (§4.3) and the only consistently gaining category (§5.1). The threat is real — fresh-DTC players (The Farmer's Dog, Ollie) proved personalization converts in pet and are taking premium dollars. This tenure spans the LLM era (ChatGPT Nov 2022), which makes a conversational intake and owner-coaching layer cheap — but the recommender itself must stay **constrained by nutritional adequacy**, not generated by a language model. The honest framing: the moat is trust + vet + scale; personalization is the mechanism, not the magic.

## 2. Problem Statement

### 2.1 The business problem

Three facts from the research file define the opportunity:

1. **Pet is the anchor and the winner.** 30% of Zone Americas sales; the only Nestlé USA category consistently gaining share; premiumization is explicitly cited as a growth lever (§12: "Purina premiumisation — wet cat, Pro Plan and veterinary channels are growing mid-single-digit RIG").
2. **The premium is under attack from personalization.** Fresh-DTC and premium challengers win on *personalization* — a diet tailored to this dog's breed, weight, age and health, on subscription. That is a direct assault on the Pro Plan / Beneful / Friskies price ladder.
3. **Pet's repurchase speed is the underexploited asset.** Grocery repurchase is weeks; pet food is a few weeks to a couple of months, with an emotionally invested buyer. That density lets a per-pet profile learn fast and lets a retention engine act while the relationship is live — an advantage grocery structurally cannot match.

### 2.2 The five technical problems

This is not "build a pet-food recommender." Five hard problems stand between a bag of kibble and defensible recurring revenue:

1. **Nutritional adequacy is a hard constraint, not a preference.** A recommendation that ignores AAFCO/FEDIAF life-stage and condition requirements, or allergens, is not a bad suggestion — it is a liability. The recommender must satisfy a nutrient-constraint system as a gate, not optimise taste or margin unconstrained.
2. **Cold start and sparse profiles.** Most pets have no rich profile; a new owner knows the dog's breed and age but not its body condition, activity, or sensitivities. The system must bootstrap from breed/age priors and learn fast from repurchase and feedback.
3. **Three stakeholders, one decision.** The *owner* buys, the *vet* recommends, the *pet* eats. A recommender optimised only for owner clicks will drift from what vets endorse and what pets thrive on; the vet channel is a first-class input, not an afterthought.
4. **Fragmented retail data and privacy.** Purchases happen across Walmart, PetSmart, Chewy, Amazon and vet clinics. First-party data is partial; retailer data is contractually restricted. The honest coverage is partial, and the project must state it rather than assume a unified profile.
5. **Retention is a causal problem, not a churn score.** A churn model tells you who *will* leave; it does not tell you which intervention *keeps* them. Retention lift must be measured with holdouts, not inferred from a score.

### 2.3 Why this was buildable in Mar 2022 – Nov 2026

| Capability | Availability in window | Use in this project |
|---|---|---|
| Gradient-boosted recommenders | LightGBM / XGBoost 1.x | Ranking, churn, propensity |
| Constraint solving | OR-Tools, linear programming | Nutrient-adequacy gate |
| Hybrid recommenders | Matrix factorisation + content | Cold-start-friendly ranking |
| LLM intake + coaching | GPT-4 / GPT-4o class, Claude (2023+) | Conversational profiling, owner coaching |
| Embeddings / retrieval | Vector stores over ingredient/KB text | Explainable, grounded coaching |
| Feature store | Feast / Tecton | Profile + behaviour features |
| Experimentation | A/B platforms, holdout design | Retention causal measurement |
| MLOps | MLflow 2.x, Airflow 2.x / Dagster | Registry, pipelines |
| Generative AI as *recommender* | **Excluded** | **Never** — constrained recommender decides |

The window is well-matched: classical recommenders and constraint solvers are mature, and LLMs make the intake/coaching layer affordable — but the discipline of *not* letting an LLM choose a pet's diet is the design decision that makes the project defensible.

## 3. Scope

### 3.1 In scope

**Pet profile**
- Species, breed, age, weight, body condition, neuter status, activity, health conditions, allergies, current diet, preferences

**Recommendation**
- Formula + daily portion across Purina Pro Plan, Pro Plan Veterinary Diets, Purina ONE, Beneful, Friskies, Beyond
- Life-stage and condition adaptation (puppy/kitten → adult → senior; weight, renal, urinary, GI, allergy)
- Vet-channel decision aid for prescription diets

**Retention and premiumization**
- Churn prediction, subscription conversion, trigger interventions, LTV
- Mix-shift measurement toward Pro Plan / wet / prescription → RIG contribution

**Outputs**
- A per-pet nutrition plan (formula + daily portion + rationale)
- Owner-facing coaching and vet-facing summary
- Retention risk score + recommended intervention
- Premiumization / RIG contribution measurement

### 3.2 Out of scope

- Veterinary diagnosis or medical treatment (the system never diagnoses; it recommends nutrition and defers to vets)
- Fresh / home-cooked food manufacturing (Nestlé is not entering the fresh-DTC cold chain here)
- Retail execution / planogram / pricing optimisation (separate pricing and supply-chain teams own that)
- Autonomous subscription billing changes (the owner decides)

### 3.3 Non-goals

- **No LLM-generated diets.** An LLM can *explain* and *coach*; it cannot *choose* a pet's food. The constrained recommender owns the decision.
- **No recommendation that violates nutritional adequacy.** AAFCO/FEDIAF and allergen constraints are a hard gate; a plan that fails them is not shown.
- **No churn score without a holdout.** Retention lift is measured causally, never asserted from a propensity score.
- **No diagnosis.** The system recommends nutrition and explicitly routes medical questions to a vet.

### 4.1 Component notes

**The constraint solver is the differentiator, not the ranker.** Most recommenders rank by predicted preference; this one ranks, then *rejects any candidate that fails nutrient adequacy or allergen constraints*. The ranker proposes; the solver disposes. This is what makes the system safe to show a pet owner and defensible to a vet.

**Petcare is the beachhead because repurchase is fast.** Grocery repurchase is measured in weeks; pet food in a few weeks to a couple of months, with an emotionally invested buyer. That density is what lets a per-pet profile learn quickly and lets a retention engine act while the relationship is live.

**The vet channel is a feature, not a channel.** Prescription diets (Pro Plan Veterinary Diets) are vet-endorsed by definition. A vet-facing summary that lets a clinician endorse or override is simultaneously a safety mechanism and a premiumization lever — vet endorsement supports premium willingness-to-pay.

**LLM is intake and coaching, never the decider.** The LLM turns messy free text ("he's a chunky lab, itchy paws, hates fish") into a structured profile and generates plain-language coaching. It never selects the diet. Grounding the coaching in the structured plan (retrieval over the KB) prevents nutritional hallucination.

**Retention is causal.** A churn score is a diagnostic; a retention *lift* requires holdout groups. The engine ships interventions (subscription nudge, portion review, vet check-in) and measures incremental retention against a randomized holdout.

## 5. Data Strategy

### 5.1 Data sources

| Source | Granularity | Use |
|---|---|---|
| First-party profile (app/web/vet) | Per pet, event | Core profile |
| Retailer loyalty / e-commerce (Chewy, Amazon, PetSmart) | Basket, SKU, date | Repurchase, premiumization |
| Vet-channel records (where permitted) | Per pet, visit | Condition, prescription, endorsement |
| Nutrition KB (AAFCO/FEDIAF, formulations) | Formula, nutrient | Constraint system |
| Owner feedback / ratings | Per plan, event | Adherence, satisfaction |
| Outcome (vet-confirmed, where available) | Per pet, time | Weak health labels |
| Subscription / retention events | Per owner, time | Churn labels |

### 5.2 The cold-start and sparse-profile problem

The project's central constraint, stated honestly: most pets have a thin profile at first contact — breed and age, rarely weight, body condition, or sensitivities. Mitigations, in order of importance:

1. **Breed/age priors** bootstrap a safe default plan before any behavioural data exists.
2. **Progressive profiling** — the intake and coaching layers fill gaps over the first orders (weight, body condition, tolerance) without interrogating the owner.
3. **Repurchase as the fastest signal** — the first reorder is a strong revealed-preference label.
4. **Explicit "insufficient profile" state** — never present a confident personalized plan when the profile cannot support it; fall back to a safe standard plan and say so.

### 5.3 The fragmented-data problem

First-party + vet + retailer data is partial and contractually bounded. The project states achievable coverage rather than assuming a unified pet graph:

- Per-retailer data used only within contract terms; no cross-retailer pooling without permission.
- Pet-owner data is personal data under US state privacy laws; consent and deletion honoured.
- The "single pet view" is aspirational; the engine degrades gracefully to first-party-only when retailer data is absent.

### 5.4 Label and ground-truth strategy

- **Retention labels** from subscription/purchase events (churn = no repurchase beyond expected depletion window).
- **Adherence** from reorder cadence vs. recommended portion (a proxy, not truth).
- **Health outcomes** are weak and vet-confirmed only where permitted — never inferred from purchases.
- **Premiumization** measured as mix shift (Pro Plan / wet / prescription) per pet over time, tied to RIG.

## 6. Evaluation

### 6.1 Metrics

| Metric | Definition | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **Incremental retention lift** | Holdout vs. treated, 90/180-day repurchase | **≥ 5% relative (measured causally)** |
| **RIG / premiumization contribution** | Mix shift toward premium SKUs, per pet | **≥ +2pp mix to Pro Plan/wet/prescription** |
| Subscription conversion | Trial → active subscription | Report vs. baseline |
| LTV lift | 12-month value, treated vs. holdout | Report |
| Nutritional-adequacy pass rate | Plans passing AAFCO/FEDIAF + allergen gate | **100% (hard gate)** |
| Vet endorsement rate | Vets who endorse vs. override recommended plan | ≥ 0.60 endorse |
| Owner satisfaction | Post-plan rating / adherence | Report |
| Cold-start safety | % of thin-profile pets given a safe standard plan | 100% never unsafe |
| Recommendation diversity | Coverage across formulas (anti-filter-bubble) | Report |

**The headline metric is causally measured incremental retention (and premiumization mix), gated by a 100% nutrient-adequacy pass rate.** A retention number from a churn score is not evidence; a premium mix that fails nutritional adequacy is a recall risk, not a win.

### 6.2 Evaluation protocol

- **Randomized holdouts for every retention intervention** — the only way a retention claim is honest.
- **Shadow mode** before any plan reaches an owner; nutrient-adequacy gate is a release blocker.
- **Per-life-stage and per-condition reporting** — a plan that works for adult maintenance and fails for renal is a half-product.
- **Vet-in-the-loop validation** for prescription diets; vet endorsement is an evaluation signal, not a formality.
- **Cold-start evaluation** on thin profiles, measuring safety first, satisfaction second.

### 6.3 Error analysis

Every failure attributed to one of:

1. **Constraint error** (a plan slipped past the adequacy gate — most severe)
2. **Cold-start error** (confident personalization on a thin profile)
3. **Preference error** (owner rejected the plan — taste, texture, price)
4. **Retention attribution error** (churn credited to intervention without a holdout)
5. **Data-coverage error** (retailer data missing → wrong premiumization read)
6. **Vet override** (clinician rejected the recommendation — a label, not only a loss)

Categories 1 and 2 are reviewed at every release. In pet nutrition, a confidently wrong plan is a safety event, not a UX miss.

## 7. Human-in-the-Loop Design

**Owner sees:** the recommended formula and daily portion, a plain-language rationale ("large-breed adult, neutered, sensitive to chicken → this formula"), a transition plan, and a clear "this is nutrition guidance, not veterinary advice" boundary.

**Vet sees:** a per-pet summary, the nutritional-adequacy rationale, the ability to endorse or override, and the adherence signal. Vets remain the medical authority.

**Actions logged as labels:** owner accept/adjust/reject (with reason); vet endorse/override (with reason). Every action is a training example for the next governed release.

**Requirement:** an owner must understand the plan in under a minute; a vet must be able to defend or override it in under two. If either cannot, the plan is not shippable.

## 10. Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **A recommendation violates nutritional adequacy** | **Critical** | OR-Tools adequacy + allergen gate as a hard release blocker; 100% pass rate required |
| **LLM hallucinates a diet or health claim** | **Critical** | LLM never recommends; grounded retrieval only; audit sampling of coaching output |
| **Overclaiming personalization as veterinary advice** | **Critical** | Explicit "nutrition, not diagnosis" boundary; vet in the loop; regulatory review of claims |
| Cold start on thin profiles | High | Breed/age priors; progressive profiling; explicit "insufficient profile" safe fallback |
| Fragmented / contractually restricted retailer data | High | Per-retailer governance; degrade to first-party; no cross-retailer pooling without permission |
| Retention lift unmeasurable (no holdout) | High | Randomized holdouts mandatory for every intervention claim |
| Vet channel distrust / override | High | Vet endorsement is a first-class input; overrides logged as labels |
| Fresh-DTC competition out-innovates on convenience | High | Compete on vet trust + scale + subscription, not on being the cheapest fresh option |
| Cannibalization across Purina price tiers | Medium | Premiumization measured explicitly; monitor Beneful/Friskies volume impact |
| Scope creep into diagnosis or medical claims | Medium | Hard boundary — nutrition guidance only |

## 11. What This Deliberately Is Not

- **Not** a veterinary diagnostic system. It recommends nutrition and routes medical questions to vets.
- **Not** an LLM recommender. The LLM coaches and profiles; the constrained solver decides.
- **Not** a fresh-food DTC business. Nestlé is not entering the cold-chain fresh market here.
- **Not** a demand-sensing system. That is the sibling `Nestle-USA-DemandSensing-ProjectDoc.md`; this is a consumer-relationship and retention system.
- **Not** a human medical device or infant-medical-advice tool. The Nutrition unit's doc owns the Gerber extension.

### Runner-up use cases (adjacent, same stack)

1. **Breed-level nutrition priors as a data product.** Package the breed/age/condition constraint system into a vet-facing reference tool; low ML complexity, high vet-channel value.
2. **Pet-food cold-chain and freshness monitoring (IoT).** Adjacent to supply chain, not this data-science function.
3. **Cat-specific hydration and urinary-health nudging.** A narrow, high-value extension of the coaching layer for the large and growing wet-cat segment.
